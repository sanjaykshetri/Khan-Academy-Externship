Partnerships analysis · Money-model shortlist

Three ways to support free learning

Potential funding and partnership models for Khan Academy, separating documented examples from proposed benefits and risks.

Model 01

Corporate-Sponsored Educational Content

Khan Academy could partner with companies to sponsor courses or educational programs in exchange for limited brand recognition. The educational content would remain free, and sponsors would have no control over the curriculum.

On the record · Real-world example

Khan Academy partnered with Bank of America to develop Better Money Habits, a free financial education initiative launched in 2013. The collaboration illustrates corporate support for freely accessible educational resources.

Our read · Proposed outcomes and risks

What Khan Academy could gain

Financial support to develop new courses, improve technology, and reach more students. Sponsorships could provide a more consistent revenue stream without charging learners.

What could go wrong

Sponsors might try to influence content to promote their interests. Too much branding could undermine Khan Academy’s reputation as an independent, student-centered platform.

Model 02

School and District Licensing

Khan Academy could offer schools and districts paid access to premium services, including advanced learning analytics, teacher dashboards, professional development, and implementation support, while keeping its core learning resources free.

On the record · Real-world example

Khan Academy operates a Khan Academy Districts program that offers district-level instructional tools, data insights, and implementation support through paid partnerships. This is an existing institutional funding approach; no specific contract amount or date is asserted here.

Our read · Proposed outcomes and risks

What Khan Academy could gain

Recurring revenue from districts and stronger relationships with educators. With appropriate privacy protections, classroom feedback and data could help improve tools and assess learning outcomes.

What could go wrong

Wealthier districts could access better tools and support than underfunded schools. Sales processes can be slow, and implementation, training, and data security create additional costs.

Model 03

Technology and AI Infrastructure Partnerships

Khan Academy could partner with technology companies that provide funding, cloud computing resources, or AI infrastructure in exchange for recognition and opportunities to collaborate on educational innovation. This could expand AI-powered learning without passing the costs on to students and teachers.

On the record · Real-world example

On May 21, 2024, Microsoft and Khan Academy announced a partnership involving donated Azure AI infrastructure to make Khanmigo for Teachers free for U.S. educators. The tool had previously cost approximately $4 per month or $44 per year for teachers.

Our read · Proposed outcomes and risks

What Khan Academy could gain

Lower technology costs, better AI tutoring capabilities, and wider access to personalized learning. Khan Academy could also benefit from partners’ technical expertise.

What could go wrong

Reliance on one technology provider could make free services difficult to sustain if support ends or prices change. Student data privacy and independence in technology decisions are also concerns.

Optional · Company worth a look

Google

Google for Education and Google Cloud could be explored for Model 3, potentially contributing cloud infrastructure, AI technology, and technical support. This is a prospective partner idea, not a claim of a proposed or agreed deal.
